Salesforce / EdgeFlow release engineering

Guarded releases
for Salesforce Edge.

Automate the repeated work. Keep release decisions tied to production health.

The risk behind every rollout

Salesforce Edge is a tier-0 traffic entry point: a faulty rollout could disrupt Salesforce globally. Regional releases required repeated configuration and test-file pull requests, dashboard checks and manual progress tracking. I originated and implemented release automation as a major use case of EdgeFlow, the general-purpose automation platform I built for engineering operations.

From preparation to deployment

I engineered the workflows to prepare releases across three repositories and multiple artifacts, trigger Jenkins builds at the end of a two-week sprint and wait for completion. Automation created Slack release threads and tracking canvases containing versions, status, change inventories and change-owner testing records.

Ready builds deployed automatically to development environments. Engineers tested their own changes before progression to pre-production.

One release process, two deployment paths

The orchestration covered 46 deployment groups across development, pre-production, canary and production regions. Separate workflows handled first-party Linux hosts running Docker Compose and public-cloud environments running Kubernetes. The release process coordinated both substrates while using the appropriate deployment workflow for each.

Readiness came from operational evidence

The system collected approximately 100 operational metrics and generated PDF reports with graphs. AI agents assessed release health and whether a rollout could proceed to the next region. Post-deployment checks compared metrics with immediate pre-deployment, prior-day and prior-week baselines.

AI health assessment, staged progression and human change-owner validation were distinct controls. Release status, reports and relevant pull requests were posted to the Slack thread, giving engineers a shared view of the work.

Stop, observe, then continue

If PagerDuty incidents appeared after deployment, automation stopped progression and notified the on-call engineer. During approximately 24-hour soak periods, it rechecked metrics and incidents every 20 minutes before further progression. Change-owner testing and on-call investigation remained part of the process.

I persisted release state in GitHub-hosted YAML, allowing workflows to update release progress and support two or three concurrent releases.

The result

Elapsed rollout time decreased from four weeks to three.

Automation took over repeated metric collection, checks and reporting. This is the release use case's outcome within EdgeFlow.

The work turned a release from repeated manual coordination into a shared, guarded operational process, with production health informing progression. Explore the EdgeFlow platform behind it.
